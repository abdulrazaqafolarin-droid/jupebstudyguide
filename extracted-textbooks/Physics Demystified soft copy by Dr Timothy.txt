Demystified Series   Physics Demystified by Dr Timothy  5  CHAPTER 1   –   MEASUREMENT, SCALARS AND VECTORS.  ●   Measurement   –   This is the estimation of the magnitude of a physical quantity. A measured quantity   consists   of   its   measured   value   or magnitude and its unit e.g the length of a rule is 1m.  ●   Fundamental and derived quantities and their units   –  - Fundamental quantities   –   These are physical quantities from which others are derived. Mass, length and time are the three main fundamental quantities. The international agreed system of units for measurements are kilogram (kg) for mass, meter (m) for length and second (s) for time. It is measured by beam   balance or chemical balance.   It works on the   principle of moments.  -   The mass of a body is constant and does not change with change in gravity.  - Measurement of weight   –   Weight is the   earth’s  gravitational pull on a body. It is measured by a  spring balance . It works on   Hooke’s law .  -   The weight of a body varies with change in gravity.  - Measurement of length   –  1. Meter rule   –   Measures length to   2d.p in cm   or  1d.p in mm   , (M ± 0.5)mm. The reading accuracy of a meter rule is   0.5mm or 0.05cm . It is used to measure short distance like the length of a book or a piece of paper, length of a wire e.t.c.  2. Vernier caliper   –   Measures small lengths to  2d.p in cm   , (M ± 0.01)cm or   1d.p in mm . The reading accuracy of a Vernier caliper is 0.1mm or 0.01cm. It is used to measure both the   internal and external diameter of round objects e.g a cylindrical test tube,   thickness of a meter rule or a plate, depth of a cavity and   diameter of a rod.  - How to read a Vernier caliper   –  i. Read the main scale till the mark just before the Vernier scale and take note of the reading in 1d.p in cm.   ii. Read the Vernier scale until the point where it meets or coincides with the Vernier scale and take note of the reading in 2d.p in cm.  iii. The reading is the sum of the main scale reading and vernier scale reading.  3.Micrometer screw guage   –   Measures very small length to 3d.p in cm or 2d.p in mm i.e (M ± 0.01)mm.   The   reading   accuracy   of   a micrometer screw guage is 0.01mm or 0.001cm. It   is   used   to   measure   external   or   outside diameter   only   of   a   round   objects   e.g cylindrical   test   tubes,   thickness   of   a   thin converging lens or pendulum bulb, thickness of a piece of paper or rod.  - How to read a micrometer screw guage   –  i. Read the sleeve scale just before the thimble scale and take note of the reading in 1d.p in mm.  ii. Read the thimble scale until the point where it meets or coincides with the sleeve scale and take note of the reding in 2d.p in mm.   iii. The reading is the sum of the sleeve reading and thimble  reading.  - Measurement of volume   –   The volume of an irregular   solid   is   measured   by   a   measuring cylinder i.e the volume of water displaced by the solid from the cylinder is equal to its volume.  ●   Error in measurement   –   The deviation of a measured value in measurement from the actual or original value. It is divided into two : kinematics and dynamics.   Kinematics   deals with the motion of a body neglecting the force which causes motion.   Dynamics   deals with the motion of a body and the force which causes the motion.  - Types of motion :  I. Translational motion   –   Motion of objects in a straight line. All points are moved in a parallel direction and with equal distance. e.g motion of a vehicle in a road .  II. Rotational motion   –   Motion of a body about the center of its axis e.g blade of a fan, wheels of moving cars.  -   The centre of a coin as it rolls down an inclined plane undergoes translational motion only while other   parts   of   the   coin   undergo   rotational motion.  -   A moving car is undergoing both rotational (the wheel of the tyre) and translational motion (the whole vehicle).  III. Oscillatory or vibrational motion   –   To and fro motion or a periodic motion of a body about a fixed point e.g simple harmonic motion, wave motion.  IV. Random motion   –   Haphazard or disordered movement   of   a   body   e.g   motion   of   gass molecules, Brownian motion.  - Position   x, y   –   It is the location of a body in a line, surface or space.  - Distance   𝒔   –   The separation between two points   or   separation   between   two   different positions of a body in motion.  - Displacement   𝒔   –   The distance of a body measured in a specified direction or distance between the initial and final position of a body in motion.  - Speed   𝒗   –   The distance covered per unit time.  - Velocity   𝒗   –   The displacement of the body per unit   time   or   speed   measured   in a   specified direction.  - Acceleration   𝒂   –   The rate of change of velocity with time.
